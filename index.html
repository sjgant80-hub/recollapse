Re-collapse is a reframing practice: you name a fixed past event, rise above the scene, write several different readings of what it meant, choose the one that opens the most future, and seal it. Every re-collapse is saved on this device and content-addressed by the event, so returning to the same memory deepens its reading rather than duplicating it. It works on the meaning you carry, not on what happened, and is not a substitute for real care.

One · Name it

What's the scene?

The thing that happened. Name it plainly — you won't edit this. The event is fixed; only its meaning is up for re-collapse.

Two · Rise

Climb above the scene.

You're not inside it now. You're looking down — it's a bounded thing you can hold and turn over, not a place you're trapped in. Take a breath. From up here you can hold the meaning open.

the fixed event · you won't edit this
Three · Walk the readings

Turn it in the light.

Each reading sits a turn further round — walk a few and you spiral out of the one wound instead of orbiting it. You don't need all of them. Write what's true for the ones that land.

Four · Collapse

Which one opens the most future?

Not the safest — the one that makes you more. This choice is yours; nothing chooses it for you. Pick the past worth carrying.
